The difference

Every answer carries a path, not a paragraph.

The engine does not read tables and guess. It traverses the school's own knowledge graph — student to risk to the clause that governs it to the policy that holds it — and it will only answer from paths the graph actually returns.

The guardrail

Irreversible actions do not leave this system.

Drafting is automatic. Sending is not. Every parent notification, every substitute assignment, every write to a student record is staged as PENDING_HUMAN_APPROVAL and waits for an officer to decide.

hours_returned = sum(unit_qty × minutes_per_unit) / 60.
estimated_value = hours_returned × that unit's role rate.

How we work with a school

Discovery, pilot, retainer.

We are an automation agency, not a licence reseller. The engagement is scoped to the problems a school is actually losing time to, and the engine is our delivery instrument — configured to your records and your policy pack, not a generic template.

STEP 01

Discovery

We sit with your operations and compliance leads, map where time actually goes, and pull your policy pack into the graph. Output: a ranked list of automations with a defensible hours estimate attached to each — before you commit to anything.

STEP 02

Pilot

One workflow, end to end, on your live data with the guardrail on. You see the staged actions, you approve or reject them, and you get the same ROI ledger this page shows. If the numbers do not hold up in your office, we stop there.

STEP 03

Retainer

The automations that earned their place go into production. We keep the policy graph current as your documents change, extend coverage, and report hours returned on a schedule you can take to your board.

Why a knowledge graph is the moat. Anyone can hire a workflow developer. Few can hand a principal a chain from a named student to the clause that governs the action, keep that chain current as policy documents change, and refuse to speak when the chain is not there. That is the work.
